Can an AI agent trained from raw pixels beat a human lap time in CarRacing-v3?

Abstract

APEX AI is a reinforcement learning project built on OpenAI Gymnasium's CarRacing-v3 environment. Our goal is to train an AI agent that can drive a simulated race car and beat a lap time set by a human player.

The agent perceives the world through a 96×96 pixel camera feed — no GPS, no map, no privileged information. A Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) extracts spatial features from each frame, which are fed into a Proximal Policy Optimisation (PPO) algorithm that decides steering, acceleration, and braking at every timestep.

We establish a human benchmark using a manual driving interface with WASD controls and a live leaderboard, then compare the trained AI agent's best lap time against the human's. Success is defined as the AI beating the human benchmark on the same track.

Discussion

Training an agent from raw pixels presents significant challenges. Without a GPU, training cycles are slow, requiring careful selection of hyperparameters to make progress within the project timeline. The sparse reward structure of CarRacing-v3 means the agent can develop suboptimal habits early in training that are difficult to correct.

We addressed spinning behaviour by applying a minimum gas floor during training, ensuring the agent always moves forward. Future work could explore reward shaping and curriculum learning to further accelerate convergence.
